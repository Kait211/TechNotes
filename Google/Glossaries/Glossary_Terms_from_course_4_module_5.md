### **New Terms and Their Definitions**

**Backup and restore:** A Microsoft first-party solution that backs up files, including a file-based option that stores files in a ZIP archive.

**Data recovery:** The process of restoring data after an unexpected event causes data loss or corruption.

**Data tapes:** A standard storage medium used for archival backup data.

**Detection measure:** Measures that alert you and your team when a disaster occurs that could affect operations.

**Differential backup:** A backup of files that have changed or been created since the last full backup.

**Disaster recovery plan:** A collection of documented procedures explaining how to respond to and handle an emergency or disaster.

**Disaster recovery testing:** A regular exercise where different teams, including IT support specialists, simulate disaster scenarios to test recovery plans.

**File compression:** The process of copying files and folders into an archive to reduce their storage size.

**Full backup:** A backup that includes the complete contents of all selected files, whether they have been modified or not.

**NAS device:** A Network Attached Storage device that connects to a network and provides centralized data storage and backups.

**Preventative measures:** Procedures or systems designed to minimize the impact of a disaster before it happens.

**Post mortem:** Documentation of problems discovered during data recovery and how they were fixed to prevent them from happening again.

**RAID (redundant array of independent disks):** A method of combining multiple physical disks into one large virtual storage system.

**Restoration procedures:** Documented and regularly tested steps that allow authorized people to restore operations when needed.

**Risk assessment:** A process used to identify and prioritize parts of an organization that are most at risk during an unexpected event.

**Single point of failure:** One part of a system that, if it fails, causes the entire system to stop working.
